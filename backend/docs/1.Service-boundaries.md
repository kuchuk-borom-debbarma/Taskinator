# Taskinator Architecture: Custom Systems & Service Boundaries

---

## 🏗️ 1. Workspace Service: The Structural Foundation

This service manages the "Skeleton" of the work. It is responsible for the integrity of the hierarchy, regardless of how complex the relationships become.

* **Standard Logic (The Core Set):** You provide the fundamental links: `Parent/Child` and `Blocked By`. These are the "physics" of the app that cannot be broken.
* **User-Defined Extensions:** Users can create custom relationship types such as `Peer Of`, `References`, or `Requires Review From`.
* **The Guardrail:** No matter what custom links are added, the service performs **Cycle Detection**. It ensures the project remains a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG), preventing "logic deadlocks" where tasks wait for themselves.
* **Parental Authority:** This service enforces the "Master Key" rule: the owner of a Parent task always retains the right to modify, bridge, or override any child relationship.

---

## 👥 2. Topology Service: Team & Permission Mapping

This service maps the human organization onto the task structure, ensuring that custom workflows respect departmental boundaries.

* **Standard Logic:** You define the basic roles—`Owner`, `Lead`, and `Member`.
* **Status-Based Access Control:** Users can restrict specific custom statuses to specific teams. For example, only the "QA Team" might be authorized to move a task into a custom `Verified` state.
* **Contextual Dialects:** Different teams can operate with different status sets. The *Dev Team* may use a 5-step status cycle, while the *Legal Team* uses a completely different 3-step approval flow.

---

## ⚡ 3. Flow Service: The Event & Automation Brain

This is the reactive layer. It translates custom status changes into automated system actions.

* **Standard Logic:** You provide the default triggers, such as "When all children are `Done`  Mark Parent `Ready`."
* **Custom State Machines:** Users define the "Rules of the Road." They determine valid transitions, such as: *"A task cannot move to `Deploy-Ready` unless it has first occupied the `Security-Audited` status."*
* **Logic Gates:** This service watches for custom status events and fires commands back to the Workspace Service. It acts as the "if/then" engine for the entire organization.

---

## 📡 4. Nexus Service: Bi-Directional Communication

This service manages **Pings**, using both standard and custom relationships to route information to the right stakeholders.

* **Standard Logic:** Provides manual "Request for Help" pings from children to direct parents.
* **Relationship-Based Routing:** Pings can be tied to custom relationships. If a task is linked via a custom `Requires Audit` relationship, a **Ping** can be automatically routed to the "Compliance Team" the moment that task hits a certain milestone.
* **Resolution Tracking:** It manages the lifecycle of these requests, ensuring that pings aren't just sent, but are "Resolved" through either child action or parent intervention.

---

## 🔍 5. Insight Service: The Transparency Read-Model

Because custom statuses can create "information silos," the Insight service provides a universal lens to see the big picture.

* **Category Mapping:** To maintain high-level dashboards, this service maps custom statuses back to your "Core Categories." Whether a task is `Drafting`, `Writing`, or `Refining`, the Insight service rolls them all up into a single `In Progress` progress bar for the parent team.
* **Cross-Hierarchy Filtering:** Users can "Drill Down" using their custom relationships, such as: *"Show me every task across the entire company that currently `Requires Design Review`."*
